# Heritage Builder — Overall Plan for HB Demo on itch.io 2026

## 1. Planning (Ideation) — Market Research

### About the Game

- PC Indie City builder strategy game
- **Title:** Heritage Builder
- **Setting:** Ancient Vietnam (14th–19th century)
- **Genre:** 2D isometric city builder
- **Focus:** Deep historical management simulation
- **Engine:** Custom engine (Rust)
- **Stage:** Planning

### Target audience

#### 👨‍💻 1. The Classic Strategy Fan

**Profile:**

- **Age:** 25–40
- **Gender:** Mostly male, but broadening
- **Playstyle:** Analytical, patient, likes optimizing systems
- **Familiar With:** SimCity 2000, Banished, Cities: Skylines, Anno 1404
- **Motivation:** Enjoys planning efficient cities, experimenting with layouts, and managing economies.
- **Spending Habit:** Buys games on Steam or GOG; values replayability and depth.

**Design implications:**

- Prioritize simulation depth, economy systems, and clear feedback loops (income, population, happiness).
- Include sandbox and challenge modes.
- Provide statistics dashboards and mod support if possible.

**Marketing angle:**

“A modern take on classic isometric city builders — manage resources, optimize layouts, and grow your dream city.”

#### 🌿 2. The Creative Relaxed Builder

**Profile:**

- **Age:** 18–35
- **Gender:** Mixed
- **Playstyle:** Aesthetic-driven; enjoys calm, cozy, sandbox experiences.
- **Familiar With:** ISLANDERS, Dorfromantik, Townscaper, Terra Nil
- **Motivation:** Express creativity, design beautiful cities, unwind after work.
- **Spending Habit:** Casual player who values art style and atmosphere over hardcore mechanics.

**Design implications:**

- Offer a relax/sandbox mode with no failure states.
- Focus on cozy visual design, music, and smooth feedback (satisfying sounds, animations).
- Provide easy screenshot or sharing features.

**Marketing angle:**

“A peaceful city builder that lets you design and grow your own world at your own pace.”

#### ⚙️ 3. The Indie Game Enthusiast / Dev-Minded Player

**Profile:**

- **Age:** 20–35
- **Gender:** Mixed
- **Playstyle:** Curious, experimental, likes supporting indie projects and innovation.
- **Familiar With:** Timberborn, Going Medieval, Factorio, RimWorld, Kingdoms and Castles
- **Motivation:** Loves discovering smart indie games with unique twists or technical innovation.
- **Spending Habit:** Follows indie devs on Twitter/YouTube; buys Early Access games.

**Design implications:**

- Make your unique hook visible early (e.g., setting, gameplay twist, or technical achievement).
- Emphasize your independent development story — people love seeing small devs build something special.
- Include modular systems or emergent behavior to encourage community engagement.

**Marketing angle:**

“An indie-built isometric city sim crafted with passion and precision — manage, build, and innovate.”

### Core concept

- **⚙️ Meaningful Systems** - Every building affects the economy, population, or environment
- **Core Loop:** Collect → Build → Optimize → Expand
- **Meta Loop:** Progression → Unlocks → City Growth → Build Monument of the Heritage
- **Must Have (core systems):** Buildings, Levels, Disaster
- **Nice to Have (expand later):** More complexity
- **Future Ideas (save for sequels or updates):** Other Cities

## 2. Pre-Production

Detailed plan

- Game Design Document (GDD)
- Blueprint of storyline, characters, game mechanics, art style, UI/UX, and monetization strategies
- Prototypes are built to test the core mechanics (the "fun factor")

## 3. Production

- Programmers write the code
- Artists and animators create visual assets
- Sound designers handle audio elements.
- Level designers coordinate flow and progression
- Project managers supervise the process.

## 4. Testing & Quality Assurance (QA)

- Find and fix bugs, glitches, or exploits.
- Ensure the game is fun, balanced, and provides a seamless player experience.

## 5. Pre-Launch

- Refine to a high standard: a stable beta version for a select audience to gather final feedback.
- Marketing efforts (trailers and demos), build hype before the release date.

## 6. Launch

The game is released to the public on itch.io (final marketing push and preparing for community engagement)

### 1. Account Requirements

- An itch.io account (free).
- Display name (your studio or personal dev name).
- Profile picture or logo
- Short bio
- Social/media links (Twitter/X, YouTube, website, etc.)

### 2. Game Build Requirements

- A playable build ready to upload.
- Supported formats: .zip, .exe, .app, .tar.gz, .love, or HTML5 (web build).

**The build should:**

- Run without extra installation steps.
- Include all required files/assets.
- Have a clear folder structure (no clutter).

**Recommended:**

- File size under 500 MB for faster download.
- Test the game on a clean system (not your dev machine).

### 3. Game Page Requirements

**Fill out your game’s page with:**

- Title
- Short description (1–2 sentences summarizing the game)
- Long description (paragraph or bullet points explaining gameplay and features)
- Game files
- **Cover image (banner)** — 630×500 px (main thumbnail).
- **Screenshots** — at least 3 high-quality images.
- **Genre/tags** — e.g. “city builder,” “simulation,” “strategy,” “isometric.”
- **Supported platforms** — Windows, macOS, Linux, or Web.
- **Status** — “Released,” “In Development,” or “Early Access.”
- **Visibility** — “Draft,” “Restricted,” or “Public.”
- **Price** — Free, Pay What You Want, or Fixed Price.

### 4. Optional but Recommended Additions

- Gameplay video or trailer (YouTube or uploaded file).
- Background image for the page.
- Readme file (controls, how to play, credits).
- Version number and update notes.
- Devlog entries to show progress.
- Community page (for comments and feedback).

### 5. Legal & Content Requirements

- You must own or have rights to all assets (code, music, art).
- No copyrighted or stolen material.
- No adult or illegal content that violates itch.io’s terms.
- Game must comply with itch.io’s Community Guidelines and Content Policy.

### 6. After Publishing

- Test your download links to confirm the build installs and runs.
- Check your game page on desktop and mobile.
- Announce your launch on social media or dev communities.
- Collect feedback and update regularly.

## 7. Post-Launch

Developers continue to support the product with bug fixes, performance patches, and potential downloadable content (DLC) based on player feedback.
