# Heritage Builder — The World

The setting of Heritage Builder is a fictional world. Three realms coexist and interact through trade, diplomacy and conflict. Each one is drawn from a real civilisation of mainland Southeast Asia. This file covers:
- the naming rules;
- the realms and the powers around them;
- the shared timeline that lets the three campaigns connect.

See [story.md](story.md) for the characters and plots, and [campaigns.md](campaigns.md) for the missions.

## Rules for the fiction

1. **No real names in the game.** No real civilisation, dynasty, city, region, river or historical person is named in the game: not in its text, voice-over, UI or store copy. Real history is reference material only.
2. **Fictional names should still sound right.** Each name is built from the language and naming habits of the culture it stands for, and echoes a real place or person without copying it. A player who knows the region should recognise the inspiration. A player who doesn't should simply find the world believable.
3. **Real history is used loosely.** Events are borrowed, compressed, moved in time or combined when the story needs it. Accuracy matters for the look and feel of daily life (architecture, crops, crafts, dress, festivals, music), not for dates or politics.
4. **Respect for living cultures.** Faiths, sacred sites and national symbols are shown with care. No realm is the villain. Conflicts are told so that each side has understandable reasons, and the same war is played from both sides in different campaigns.
5. **Reference names stay in the design docs.** The real-world references in the tables below are for the team only. They never ship.

### Name style per realm

| Realm | Language flavour | Writing in game | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Đại Long | Vietnamese, Sino-Vietnamese titles | Vietnamese diacritics kept (the UI font must support them) | Thăng Hà, Khai Sơn, Sông Son |
| Kambara | Old Khmer with Sanskrit titles | Latin script, light romanisation, no diacritics in the UI | Yasonagara, Sovanna, Sūryadeva → "Suryadeva" in the UI |
| Tampadesa | Burmese with Pali titles | Latin script, light romanisation | Pugara, Thiha, Shwemyit |

## The three realms

All names below are **working names**, open to change.

### Đại Long — the Realm of the Great Dragon

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reference** | Đại Việt under the Lý and early Trần dynasties (11th–13th centuries) |
| **Capital** | Old capital: Hoa Thành (ref. Hoa Lư). New capital, founded in Campaign I: Thăng Hà (ref. Thăng Long, Hà Nội) |
| **Heartland** | The delta of the Sông Son, the "Cinnabar River" (ref. Red River), with limestone karst to the south |
| **Ruling house** | The Long, the "Dragon Line" (fictional) |
| **Landscape** | Rice paddies on flood plains, dykes, bamboo-ringed villages, karst towers, highland passes, a long coast |
| **Architecture** | Dark timber frames, terracotta tile roofs with curved ridges, red lanterns, ochre walls, communal village halls, multi-tier gate towers (see `images/references/citadel_vn.png`, `factory_vn.png`) |
| **Economy** | Wet rice, fish, silk, ceramics, salt, bronze, timber, lacquer |
| **Society** | Village communes under scholar-officials chosen by examination, with a court of nobles above them |
| **Faith (fictionalised)** | Ancestor veneration, village guardian spirits, and a contemplative temple tradition (ref. Mahāyāna Buddhism mixed with Confucian ethics) |
| **Legitimacy idea** | **The Mandate** (ref. Thiên mệnh, the Mandate of Heaven): a ruler governs only as long as Heaven, and the people, find him virtuous |
| **Music** | Đàn bầu, đàn tranh, court ensembles, water-puppet theatre music |

### Kambara — the Kingdom of the Temple Mountain

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reference** | The Khmer Empire of the Angkor period (9th–13th centuries) |
| **Capital** | Yasonagara (ref. Yaśodharapura, Angkor) |
| **Heartland** | Tonlé Thom, the "Breathing Lake" (ref. Tonlé Sap), which floods each wet season and shrinks in the dry; the sacred sandstone hills of Phnom Rumsay (ref. Phnom Kulen) |
| **Ruling house** | God-kings who rule as earthly images of the divine: King Suryadeva, later King Jayadeva (both fictional) |
| **Landscape** | Lake plains, monsoon forest, laterite soil, vast reservoirs (barays) and moats, causeways, stilt houses |
| **Architecture** | Grey sandstone and red laterite temple-mountains, lotus-bud towers (prang), galleries, moats with balustrade bridges, wooden stilt houses with thatch roofs (see `citadel_khr.png`, `market_khr.png`, `mock_khr.jpg`) |
| **Economy** | Rice from reservoir irrigation, freshwater fish, sandstone, iron, palm sugar, cardamom and spices, elephants for labour |
| **Society** | Temple estates, royal officials, and a large free and bonded labour force that builds the temples and waterworks |
| **Faith (fictionalised)** | A court cult of the Devas (ref. Hindu Shaivism and Vaishnavism) that gives way to the Compassionate Path (ref. Mahāyāna Buddhism) during Campaign II |
| **Legitimacy idea** | **Divine Favour** (ref. devarāja): the king and his works must mirror the order of the heavens |
| **Music** | Pinpeat-style ensembles: xylophone, gong circles, oboes, drums, temple dance |

### Tampadesa — the Copper Land

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reference** | The Pagan (Bagan) Kingdom (11th–13th centuries) |
| **Capital** | Pugara (ref. Pagan, Arimaddanapura) |
| **Heartland** | The dry plain on the bend of the Shwemyit, the "Golden River" (ref. Irrawaddy), with the irrigated rice valley of Kyautthin (ref. Kyaukse) to the south |
| **Ruling house** | Warrior kings who become great patrons of the faith: King Mahathura, later King Narathiri (both fictional) |
| **Landscape** | Dry plains with toddy palms and thorn scrub, the wide river, irrigated valleys, teak-forested hills, a delta coast to the south |
| **Architecture** | Red brick temples and stupas, gilded spires, whitewashed stucco, carved teak monasteries, brick city walls (see `citadel_burma.png`, `shrine_burma.png`, `mock_burma.jpg`) |
| **Economy** | Irrigated rice, sesame and cotton, bricks, lacquerware, teak, gems and jade from the hills, river trade |
| **Society** | The king, his ministers and a merit-seeking nobility, monasteries that hold large tax-free lands, and village farmers |
| **Faith (fictionalised)** | The Teaching of the Elders (ref. Theravāda Buddhism), brought from the southern ports, beside older spirit worship (ref. nat worship) |
| **Legitimacy idea** | **Merit** (ref. kutho, dhammarāja): a king proves his worth by the merit of his works, above all temples and gifts to the monastic order |
| **Music** | Drum-circle and gong-circle ensembles, bamboo clappers, harp |

## Other powers (not playable)

These powers appear on the world map as trade partners, rivals, raiders or invaders.

| Power | Reference | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Vijara**, the Sea Kingdom | Champa (Vijaya) | A trading and seafaring kingdom on the coast between Đại Long and Kambara. It is a trade partner in Campaign I, a raider on Đại Long's southern shore, and the invader that sacks Yasonagara in Campaign II. It is shown as proud and mercantile, not evil. |
| **Hamsara**, the Delta Ports | The Mon city-states (Thaton, Hamsavati/Bago) | Learned port cities south of Tampadesa that keep the scriptures of the Teaching. Hamsara is Kambara's trade partner, then becomes a province of Tampadesa at the start of Campaign III. Thiha, the protagonist of Campaign III, was born there. |
| **The Northern Throne** | The Song dynasty of China | A vast empire to the north of Đại Long. It is the source of fine trade goods and of the tribute system, and invades Đại Long in Campaign I. |
| **The Steppe Khaganate** | The Mongol Empire (Yuan) | A distant power that rises late in the timeline. Its armies reach Tampadesa in the final missions of Campaign III. It is the one threat all three realms face together. |
| **The highland clans** | Upland peoples across the region | Small trade partners, sources of forest goods, horses and elephants, and sometimes raiders on mountain missions. |
| **The far traders** | Merchants from India, Persia, Java and the islands | They arrive in the port missions to buy silk, ceramics, spices and gems. |

## The world map

The world map works as the strategic screen in Emperor and Zeus. It shows:
- the cities the player founded in earlier missions of the same campaign;
- the other realms' cities;
- the routes between them: rivers, coasts and overland roads.

In rough geography, north is at the top:

```
                 The Steppe Khaganate (far north, appears late)
                            The Northern Throne
  [Tampadesa]                          [Đại Long]
   Pugara on the Shwemyit                 Thăng Hà on the Sông Son
   Kyautthin valley                       karst south, highlands west
        |   hill roads                             | coast
        |                [highland clans]          v
   [Hamsara] delta ports             [Kambara]           [Vijara] sea kingdom
        \___ sea lanes ___ Yasonagara on Tonlé Thom ___/
```

Exact positions, distances and route types are decided when the world map is designed.

## Shared timeline

All three campaigns use one fictional calendar for design purposes: the **World Year (WY)**. In the game, each realm counts years its own way: Đại Long by reign eras, Kambara and Tampadesa by their own eras. The game shows that realm's date and the season. The real periods are compressed and reordered so the three realms meet at the right moments.

| World Year | Đại Long | Kambara | Tampadesa | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emperor Long Thái reigns from Hoa Thành. Khai Sơn is sent to the delta frontier. **Campaign I begins.** | King Suryadeva comes to the throne. | Small warring city-states on the dry plain. | |
| 5–20 | Frontier cities founded; dykes raised on the Sông Son (C-I 1–5). | The reservoirs at Yasonagara are begun. | | Vijara trades with all its neighbours. |
| 20–28 | The port at Hội Thủy opens. The Temple of Learning is founded (C-I 6–7). | **Campaign II begins (WY 22).** Lady Sovanna is given the lake provinces. | The city-states of the plain fight among themselves. | |
| 28–32 | The Emperor dies. Prince Long Uy is crowned. **Border war with Kambara** (C-I 8). | The same war, seen from the other side, ends in a negotiated peace (C-II 4). | | |
| 32–36 | **The Northern Throne invades** and is stopped at the river of stakes. Khai Sơn is acclaimed; the capital moves to Thăng Hà. **Campaign I ends.** | The temple-mountain of Suryagiri is completed (C-II 5). | | |
| 36–60 | Đại Long consolidates under its new dynasty. | King Suryadeva dies; weak heirs follow (C-II 6). | Mahathura takes the throne (WY 55) and unites the plain. | |
| 60–62 | | **Vijara's fleet sails up the lake and sacks Yasonagara** (C-II 7). | | |
| 62–80 | The aged King Khai Sơn sends grain to Kambara's refugees, and dies, honoured, around WY 70. His heir reigns. | Jayadeva returns. Yasonagara is rebuilt as a walled city and the Temple of Four Faces is raised. **Campaign II ends (around WY 80).** | Mahathura conquers Hamsara (WY 68) and carries its scriptures and scholars, Thiha among them, to Pugara. **Campaign III begins (WY 70).** | |
| 80–120 | Đại Long prospers under Khai Sơn's descendants. | Kambara at peace, trading with Tampadesa. | The great age of temple-building. The treasury is drained by tax-free temple lands (C-III 3–8). | The Steppe Khaganate rises. |
| 120–130 | Đại Long sends engineers north to Tampadesa. | Kambara sends supplies. | **The Khaganate invades.** The three realms' aid helps Pugara hold. **Campaign III and the game end.** | |

### Moments where the campaigns meet

- **The border war (WY 28–32):** it is a defence mission in Campaign I (mission 8), and in Campaign II (mission 4) the player defends against the same war and then makes peace. Each side's story shows the other as rivals, not monsters.
- **Merchants who recur:** a Vijara merchant house and a Hamsara trading family appear in more than one campaign, getting older as the years pass.
- **Grain for refugees (WY 62):** a trade request from Đại Long is part of Campaign II's recovery missions.
- **The Three Crowns (WY 120–130):** in the last mission, the player calls on the trade routes and relationships of all three realms against the Khaganate.
- **The steles:** each campaign ends with a stone stele inscribed with the governor's deeds, a tradition all three cultures share. The three steles together frame the game's ending. See [story.md](story.md#framing-device-the-three-steles).

## Real-world reference notes (team only)

These are starting points for research, never for in-game text.

## Open questions

- **The names:** do the working names (Đại Long, Kambara, Tampadesa, Thăng Hà, Yasonagara, Pugara) work, or do you want other options?
- **The faiths:** should they stay recognisable but unnamed, as written here, or become fully fictional pantheons?
- **The Steppe Khaganate:** is a Mongol-inspired common enemy at the end of the game the finale you want, or should Campaign III end on a conflict between the realms?
