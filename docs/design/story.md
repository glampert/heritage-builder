# Heritage Builder — Story

All names are fictional; see [world.md](world.md) for the naming rules, the realms and the shared timeline. Mission details are in [campaigns.md](campaigns.md), and the comic scripts in [story-script.md](story-script.md).

## The shared premise

Every campaign starts the same way. **You are a governor.** Your realm sends you to the edge of its world to found new cities, feed and house its people, open trade routes and raise monuments. Your works are meant to outlast you, as the realm's heritage.

Each campaign tells that premise through a different person, in a different time, with a different question at its heart:

| Campaign | Governor | The question |
|---|---|---|
| I. The Dragon Legacy | Khai Sơn, adopted son of the Emperor | *Is the right to rule inherited, or earned?* |
| II. The Temple Mountain | Lady Sovanna, royal water-engineer | *What is greatness for: the glory of kings, or the care of the people?* |
| III. The Plain of Ten Thousand Temples | Thiha, a scholar from a conquered land | *When does devotion become vanity, and what truly endures?* |

Some rules hold across all three stories:
- **Building, not conquest.** The governors win through the cities they build. Battles are defensive and short. The climax of each campaign is a work of construction or an act of holding on, never a victory march.
- **No realm is the villain.** Đại Long and Kambara fight a border war that the player sees from both sides. Vijara sacks Yasonagara for reasons the story explains, and later trades with it again. The one common threat is the distant Steppe Khaganate, at the very end.
- **Antagonists are human.** The rivals at court are proud, frightened or flattered, not evil. Most of them are given a way out.
- **People carry over.** Merchants, envoys and descendants link the campaigns, so the world feels continuous.

## Framing device: the three steles

All three cultures carved the deeds of rulers and builders on stone steles. Each campaign is framed as its stele being carved:
- the mission briefing opens with the stele's next lines;
- each mission's results add a line, with optional distinctions adding more;
- the end of each campaign shows the finished stele.

In the game's epilogue, centuries pass. Travellers from far lands walk among the dykes of Thăng Hà, the towers of Yasonagara and the temples of Pugara, and read the three steles standing side by side: *"They built for those who came after them."*

## Campaign I — The Dragon Legacy (Đại Long, WY 1–36)

### Cast

| Character | Role |
|---|---|
| **Khai Sơn** | The protagonist. Son of General Phan Trung, who died defending the throne, and adopted and raised by the Emperor. Earnest, practical and humble; believes a ruler is measured by full granaries, not by his ancestry. Ages from about 20 to 55 over the campaign. |
| **Emperor Long Thái** | An ageing, wise ruler. He loves Khai Sơn but cannot quiet the court. He sends him to the frontier both to protect him and to let him prove himself. Dies at WY 28. |
| **Prince Long Uy** | The First Son, true-born of the Dragon Line. Proud and insecure, with real ability, but eaten by the fear of being outshone. Crowned at WY 28. |
| **Chancellor Đoàn Kỳ** | Long Uy's flatterer. He pushes the levies, the border war and the move against Khai Sơn. |
| **Master Văn Lễ** | An elderly scholar and Khai Sơn's tutor. The voice of the tutorial and the advisor on culture and learning. |
| **Ngọc Lan** | The head of a merchant guild from the southern coast. Sharp, funny and pragmatic. Trade advisor from mission 5. |
| **Commander Hà Vũ** | A veteran of Phan Trung's guard. Defence advisor; leads the militia. |
| **Lady Sovanna** (cameo) | Kambara's envoy at the end of the border war, and the protagonist of Campaign II. |

### Synopsis

**Act 1, the Frontier (missions 1–5, WY 1–20).** At court they whisper, "He is not of the Dragon Line." To quiet them, the Emperor sends Khai Sơn to the empty flood plains of the Sông Son. There he must build without asking for favours.
- Guided by Master Văn Lễ, he settles a village of farmers, fishers and potters.
- He learns the river's rhythm, the Water Lord's yearly anger and the Mountain Lord's patience, and raises the first dykes.
- He opens a highland market town that links the delta to the hill clans.

The Emperor names him **Governor of the Southern Provinces**. Prince Long Uy watches the frontier's fame grow, and so does his resentment.

**Act 2, Shadows at Court (missions 6–8, WY 20–32).**
- Khai Sơn opens the port of Hội Thủy to the sea traders. He is the first to meet Vijara's merchant houses and Vijara's pirates.
- He founds the **Temple of Learning**, where commoners can rise by examination. Many at court take this as an insult.
- The Emperor falls ill and dies. Long Uy is crowned, and Chancellor Đoàn Kỳ's new levies fall hardest on Khai Sơn's provinces.
- When the court demands grain from villages ruined by a flood, Khai Sơn pays the levy from his own treasury rather than strip his people. It is a gameplay choice, and it quietly defies the throne.
- Long Uy's court provokes a border war with Kambara. Khai Sơn defends the western marches. He refuses to raid in return, and agrees a truce with Kambara's envoy, Lady Sovanna.

**Act 3, the Ascending Dragon (missions 9–10, WY 32–36).**
- The Northern Throne takes the realm's turmoil as its chance and invades down the rivers. The new King and his court flee the old capital.
- Khai Sơn and Commander Hà Vũ drive stakes into the estuary at low tide, fortify the riverbanks and hold. The invasion breaks on the river of stakes.
- Humiliated, the King blames Khai Sơn and sends the Royal Legion south to arrest him for treason. On the road the Legion passes through three of Khai Sơn's cities, and the people come out to meet it: thousands of farmers, scholars and merchants, unarmed, with rice and tea. The soldiers will not march through them. (This is the "Silent Siege", told as a comic between missions 9 and 10.)
- The court abandons Đoàn Kỳ. Long Uy, shamed but not destroyed, gives up the throne and withdraws to a mountain monastery. Khai Sơn visits him there before he is crowned.
- The court and the people acclaim Khai Sơn. His first edict moves the capital to the delta, to Thăng Hà, the "Ascending River", and his last mission builds the **Imperial Citadel**.

**Ending.** *"The people remember his walls not for their height, but for the peace they sheltered."* Khai Sơn becomes the founder of a new line, chosen and not born. In later campaigns he appears as an old king who sends grain to Kambara's refugees, and his descendants send engineers north in the final war.

## Campaign II — The Temple Mountain (Kambara, WY 22–80)

### Cast

| Character | Role |
|---|---|
| **Lady Sovanna** | The protagonist. From a family of royal water-engineers, she is made governor of the lake provinces by King Suryadeva. Brilliant, exacting and proud at first, she learns compassion through loss. Ages from about 25 to 80. |
| **King Suryadeva** | An ambitious god-king who wants a temple-mountain to mirror the heavens. Generous to builders and hard on everyone else. Dies at WY 38. |
| **Achar Vama** | The old master-builder of the temple workshops and Sovanna's mentor. The advisor voice for monuments. Dies soon after Suryagiri is consecrated. |
| **Prince Jayadeva** | A young royal cousin: gentle, scholarly and drawn to the Compassionate Path. He goes into exile in Vijara when a usurper takes the throne, and returns as the king who restores the realm. |
| **Lord Vrah Sena** | The minister who seizes power after Suryadeva's heirs fail. Greedy, neglects the lake fleet and squeezes the provinces. |
| **The House of Ja** | A Vijara merchant family, first met in Campaign I and trade partners here. Their younger son, **Ja Kumar**, warns Sovanna of the coming fleet. |
| **Admiral Pô Rinda** | Vijara's commander, who sails up the Breathing Lake. He seeks revenge for years of Kambara's raids on Vijara's coast. |

### Synopsis

**Act 1, the Golden Age (missions 1–5, WY 22–38).**
- Sovanna is given the lake provinces. She builds stilt cities that live with the Breathing Lake, then **barays** and canals that give a second harvest in the dry season.
- She opens the sandstone quarries on the holy hills and floats stone down the canals to the capital.
- She builds the royal road towards the east. There the border war with Đại Long, the same war as in Campaign I, tests her. She defends, then rides out to make the truce with Khai Sơn's envoy, against the King's wishes.
- In the end she completes King Suryadeva's dream: **Suryagiri**, the temple-mountain, the greatest work of the age.

**Act 2, the Fall (missions 6–7, WY 38–62).**
- Suryadeva dies. His heirs quarrel, and Lord Vrah Sena takes the throne. Jayadeva flees into exile, of all places to Vijara.
- Under Vrah Sena the provinces are bled for gold and the lake fleet rots. Sovanna governs a coastal frontier on almost nothing.
- Ja Kumar brings word that Vijara's admiral is gathering ships. The court ignores her.
- In WY 60 the fleet sails up the Breathing Lake at the height of the flood and **sacks Yasonagara**. Sovanna holds a lakeside city against the raiders and shelters the refugees while the capital burns on the horizon.

**Act 3, the Restoration (missions 8–10, WY 62–80).**
- Jayadeva returns from exile, gathers the provinces, and Vrah Sena's rule collapses.
- He makes Sovanna his chief governor, with one order: *"Rebuild for the living."* She founds a city of **hospitals and rest houses** along the roads, taking in refugees. Grain arrives from Đại Long, sent by the old King Khai Sơn.
- She rebuilds Yasonagara as a great **walled and moated city** and holds it against Vijara's last assault.
- At its heart she raises the **Temple of Four Faces**, whose serene faces look out in every direction.

**Ending.** Jayadeva chooses not to take revenge on Vijara, and the House of Ja's ships return to the lake as traders. The aged Sovanna's stele reads: *"The faces smile on the city that forgave."*

## Campaign III — The Plain of Ten Thousand Temples (Tampadesa, WY 70–130)

### Cast

| Character | Role |
|---|---|
| **Thiha** | The protagonist. A scribe and engineer born in the Hamsara ports. He is brought to Pugara as a captive scholar, together with the scriptures, after King Mahathura conquers his homeland. An outsider who rises through ability. Quiet, devout and curious. Ages from about 20 to 80. |
| **King Mahathura** | The warrior king who unites the plain and takes up the Teaching of the Elders. Hard but just; he values Thiha. Dies at WY 95. |
| **King Narathiri** | Mahathura's grandson. Pious, magnificent and vain: he wants a temple on every field. On the throne when the Khaganate arrives. |
| **The Venerable Shin Dhammika** | A senior monk from Hamsara and Thiha's teacher. The advisor voice for faith and merit, and the conscience of the campaign. |
| **Treasurer Maung Pe** | The court's treasurer. Grumbling, honest and usually right about money; the voice that counters the temple frenzy. |
| **Arslan Noyan** | The Steppe Khaganate's general. Disciplined, curious about the lands he invades, and in the end practical enough to withdraw. |
| **Phan Minh** | Khai Sơn's great-grandson, a river engineer sent from Đại Long in the final war. |
| **Lady Mealea** | A physician from Kambara's royal hospitals, a student in Sovanna's line, sent with supplies in the final war. |

### Synopsis

**Act 1, the Captive Scholar (missions 1–4, WY 70–85).**
- Thiha arrives in Pugara as a prize of war. To prove his worth, Mahathura gives him a ruined village on the dry plain.
- Thiha learns to coax water from the Golden River and the hill streams. He builds weirs in the Kyautthin valley that make it the realm's granary.
- He fires the bricks that will cover the plain.
- When Mahathura obtains a sacred relic, Thiha builds the **Gilded Stupa** to house it. The King frees him and makes him a governor.

**Act 2, the Golden Plain (missions 5–8, WY 85–112).**
- Thiha opens the hill roads to the gem mines and to Đại Long and Kambara.
- He returns to his homeland, Hamsara, as its governor. There he must win over his own people, who see him as a collaborator, while serving the realm that conquered them.
- He builds the **Temple of Infinite Wisdom** for the court.
- After Mahathura dies, King Narathiri and the nobles compete to earn merit with ever more temples. Each one takes land and labour off the tax rolls, until the treasury is empty and a drought brings famine.
- With Shin Dhammika's help, Thiha must persuade the monasteries, the court and the King to find a balance between merit and survival. The player can choose how, among several options in the mission.

**Act 3, the Northern Storm (missions 9–10, WY 120–130).**
- The Steppe Khaganate, having conquered the north, sends envoys demanding submission. Narathiri insults them, and the Khaganate marches.
- Thiha, now old, fortifies the northern frontier fortress and holds the first invasion long enough for the court to prepare.
- In the last mission the Khaganate's army comes down the river towards Pugara. The trade routes and friendships of all three realms now matter: Phan Minh brings Đại Long's river-stake engineers, and Lady Mealea brings Kambara's physicians and grain.
- Pugara holds through the wet season. Fevers and the long supply line wear down the invaders. Arslan Noyan, looking out over thousands of temples, chooses to withdraw.

**Ending.** Thiha's last work is finished. The plain is covered in temples, some grand and some humble, built by kings, merchants and farmers. The final stele reads: *"Merit is not in the height of the spire, but in the hands that raised it."* The epilogue then shows the three steles standing together.

## Open questions

- **Khai Sơn's ending:** in this version he becomes king through acclamation and Long Uy's abdication, not through a rebellion. Is that the right tone, or do you want the original comic's open defiance, where he tears up the decree and fights the guards?
- **Protagonists' genders:** does the mix (male, female, male) suit you?
- **Choices:** should players make real story choices with consequences, such as how Thiha resolves the temple-lands crisis, or should story choices stay small and local to a mission?
